Gurney’s Pitta

Hydrornis gurneyi

Functionally Extinct (TH)

Gurney’s Pitta was, until 1986, considered probably extinct until a small population was found in a few remaining patches of lowland forest in the south of Thailand. However, since its rediscovery it has continued to decline in Thailand and it is considered functionally extinct at the one remaining site in Thailand – Khao Nor Chu Chi. It would seem that a few birds hang on here and there in the south of Thailand but with so little habitat and any remaining birds isolated from each other they have no future in Thailand without reforestation projects and reintroduction.

Spoon-billed Sandpiper

Calidris pygmaea

Critically Endangered

Spoon-billed Sandpipers are in such rapid decline that the population estimate of 240-500 mature individuals is almost certainly very optimistic, some ornithologists suggest that there are fewer than 200 left. This species is unique amongst wader species in having a spatulate bill which it uses in a plucking and a sieving motion to find invertebrates in mudflats and salt pans.

California Condor

Gymnogyps californianus

Critically Endangered

Once reduced to only 22 birds in 1987, the California Condor is one of the world's most endangered apex scavengers. Through intensive sanctuary breeding programs, populations now exceed 500 birds across North America, though lead bullet toxicity remains an urgent threat.

Quick Scenarios:

Bird Hit a Window (Concussion/Shock)

Critical Shock Care

Impact with glass causes acute intracranial pressure, swelling, and shock.

  • Gentle Containment: Pick up with a soft towel; place in a ventilated dark shoebox.
  • Controlled Heat: Rest half the container over a heating pad set to LOW.
  • NEVER force fluids: A concussed bird will aspirate water into its lungs immediately.
  • Reassess in 2 hours: If responsive, release in a sheltered outdoor shrub away from windows.
Offering food or drops of water to stunned birds is the leading cause of fatal inhalation pneumonia.

Found a Baby Bird on the Ground

Feathering Check

Distinguish between an unfeathered nestling and a flight-learning fledgling.

  • Nestling (Pink or sparse fluff): Cannot hop or perch. Return to nest or create a makeshift hanging basket nearby.
  • Fledgling (Fully feathered, short tail): Hops and flutters. Parents are foraging nearby. Keep pets away and observe.
  • Human Scent is Safe: Birds have virtually no sense of smell; parents will happily care for a returned baby.

Caught by a Cat or Dog

Pasteurella Lethal Risk

Feline saliva transmits *Pasteurella multocida* bacteria which causes septicemia within 24–48 hours.

  • Emergency Antibiotics Required: Even without visible punctures, microscopic scratches prove lethal without veterinary care.
  • Safe Transport: Secure in a warm, padded, dark container and take to a wildlife rehab center immediately.

Active Bleeding or Fractured Wing

Avian Vet Emergency

Birds hold minimal blood volume; severe bleeding requires direct pressure with sterile gauze.

  • Direct Pressure: Apply gentle sterile gauze or styptic powder/cornstarch to stop localized bleeding.
  • Do Not Splint at Home: Avoid amateur splints; wrap the bird loosely in a tea towel to prevent wing flapping until veterinary arrival.

avian positive reinforcement training

Science-backed, fear-free training protocols to build voluntary cooperation and mental engagement.

Level 1 • Core

Voluntary Step-Up Protocol

Empower your companion bird to step onto your arm willingly through positive reinforcers.

  • Identify highest-value treats (sunflower seed, pine nut, or almond slice).
  • Position forearm steady at bird's chest level without crowding.
  • Lure forward; click/mark the second both feet step on the perch.
Level 2 • Precision

Target Stick Conditioning

Guide birds into transit carriers, playstands, and scales safely without grab handling.

  • Present a wooden target ball gently near the beak.
  • Reward instant gentle contact with a clicker and treat delivery.
  • Gradually navigate the bird across different perches and environments.
Level 3 • Flight

Indoor Recall & Safety Cue

Train flighted birds to return reliably on acoustic cues for exercise and safety.

  • Initiate short hops from perch to hand across 12 inches.
  • Pair with a distinct 2-tone whistle or verbal cue.
  • Expand distance across open living spaces with jackpot rewards.
